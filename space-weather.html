How does EarthPulse track space weather?

EarthPulse is a source-aware planetary intelligence interface that can organize solar and near-Earth space-weather observations into one readable workflow. When supported feeds are connected, it can surface signals such as solar activity, solar-wind conditions and geomagnetic measurements with timestamps, source context and explicit labels for observed versus derived information.

SOLAR ACTIVITYGEOMAGNETIC CONTEXTSOURCE + FRESHNESS

What EarthPulse does

  • Brings multiple space-weather signals into a common visual and analytical layer.
  • Separates direct observations from EarthPulse-derived indicators and demonstrations.
  • Uses timestamps and source labels so users can judge freshness and provenance.
  • Connects space-weather signals to the wider Earth-system context instead of isolating them on a single chart.
  • Shows data gaps explicitly rather than filling them with invented values.

Common information groups

Solar activity

Useful for understanding changes in solar output and active regions.

Solar wind

Describes conditions in the charged-particle environment around Earth.

Geomagnetic activity

Helps describe how the near-Earth environment responds to solar forcing.

Context

Links space-weather observations to time, source and Earth-impact context.

Interpretation rule

EarthPulse can explain and visualize a space-weather signal, but the upstream scientific source remains the authority for the underlying observation or official alert. Any EarthPulse score or combined indicator should be treated as an analytical layer, not an official space-weather measurement.
